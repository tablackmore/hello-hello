Chapter 27

God - The Biggest Question?

Pages 459–462

Does God want all Modern Humans (MH) to know that He exists?

Some background:

First, bear in mind the typical description of the monotheistic, Christian God. It is claimed He has amazing qualities and abilities. He is fair and kind to everyone, everywhere, i.e. omni-benevolence, omnipresence. Abrahamic faiths believe that God is also transcendent, (outside of space and time) ergo He is not subject to anything arising from what He has created. That’s not all, of particularly interest is the claim of God’s omniscience and omnipotence. In addition to all this, because of His transcendence, He is eternal. In essence, we have a being who knows everything and is super-supremely powerful and capable, nothing is impossible for Him and he acts justly, wisely and kindly. If this God wants something to happen, it happens, every time, end of story.

Next, who are these ‘Modern Humans’, referred to in the question above? Well, they are us, and they are all our ancestors, right back to the very beginning of the Homo Sapiens line, some 300,000 years ago. If the answer is “Yes” to the question above, all these MH, every last one of them, has to have undeniable knowledge of a monotheistic God and know clearly what He wants of them. That’s some background, now to explore the potential answer “Yes” and the potential answer “No” to this question: ‘Does God want all Modern Humans (MH) to know that He exists?’

Try “Yes” as an answer:

This has to be the answer that Christian theologians and Christian church chiefs of every denomination would give. It’s impossible for them to answer ‘No’, after their attempted justification of the Bible, and their promotion of their Christian faith. There are huge problems if attempting to claim the answer is “YES” to the question above. Quite simply, the evidence does not support such a conclusion, by a country mile. Theses issues similarly apply to the two other Abrahamic religions, Islam and Judaism.

99% of the period of time that MH have existed, came before the Abrahamic God made his Biblical appearance. Pre-historic humans, for more than 290,000 years, mostly held beliefs that had nothing to do with a single God. Their beliefs were associated with the natural world and ancestors, some held special reverence for cave bears. None of these beliefs are anything like that

p.460

of the one God promoted by the Bible. The early historic humans, who came after their prehistoric ancestors, had a wide range of deities. From more than 7,000 years ago, civilisation’s early front-runners, Sumerians and Egyptians, paid homage to a huge pantheon of anthropomorphic Gods and Goddesses.

Later civilisations did the same. A big shift to just the one God began with the Roman Emperor, Constantine the Great, He started promoting Christianity 1,700 years ago, this was mainly to help unify his Empire. The monotheistic God was not in sight up until him, except for a tiny minority of the world’s population.

Even in the shortish time - less than 1% of total human existence - since Abraham and Constantine, many humans have either not known of, or not believed in, the monotheistic God. These are the Buddhists, Hindus, Sikhs, those of many other faiths, and of hundreds of million who have no faith at all. Atheists and agnostics have steadily increased in numbers since the age of enlightenment, 250 years ago.

In conclusion it is abundantly clear that it’s incorrect to say that God wants modern humans to know that he exists, because, if he did, he has failed by a very large margin, and God does not do failure we are told. That’s a conundrum for monotheistic religions. Therefore, in answer to the question: 'Does God want us, Modern Humans, to know that He exists, “Yes” seems not to be the answer. The clear lack of evidence says so. If not Yes, then the answer must be No, yes? Well, no, as we will see next.

Now try “No” as an answer:

‘NO’ definitely does not work for Christian faiths. At their very base is the concept of a God who is constantly involved with humans and through sacred texts has given His message. This God is a personal one who can be appealed to and prayed to. These believers cannot say that their God does not want his existence to be known. Of course, there are some deists who might say ‘ah yes, God quick-started the universe, then was no longer involved in what happened subsequently. He just stood back and watched from the sidelines, but He’s still there all right.’ That means he did not want people to know he existed. This horse falls at the very first fence. If a supremely able God did not want modern humans to know he exists, then why do billions think that he does? With his alleged powers He could ensure MH are unaware of his existence, but billions think they know of Him. Clearly, ‘No’ does not work. It is not the right answer to the question.

p.461

The correct and only answer:

Because a God as traditionally described is so unimaginably mighty He can arrange things to his own wishes. Humans cannot out-think, nor outfox Him, this, the claimed creator. It’s a puzzle then, since neither a ‘Yes’ or ‘No’ answer works. We’ve reached a point where Conan Doyle’s great fictional detective, Sherlock Holmes, would say, “When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth.” In this case what remains is not so improbable - that there is no God. That’s the only way to answer the question correctly. The question itself is based on a false premise - that a monotheistic God exists. When that premise is eliminated, God disappears, this seems to be the only reasonable conclusion that can be reached.

So, the question does not seem to work because it’s based on the assumption of a God, but what do theologians say in answer to it?

p.462

Answers given by some theologians:

The question has been asked of some top church believers who were first reminded that modern humans had been around for 300,000 years.

Professor Alister McGrath, Christian theologian, was asked the question at a lecture he gave in Cambridge. He paused before responding, then said, ‘That’s a very good question.’ After pausing again, he said to the questioner “Do you know of Plato’s Cave?” The questioner nodded yes, but Alister went on anyway to explain. His point was that we cannot understand God’s will and that we have to have ‘faith’. The same question, with the same preamble, was asked in a Cambridge debate of Archbishop Rowan Williams. He was under time pressure and clearly thought he did not have much time to give a detailed answer. He replied swiftly, and without any supporting argument said, “Yes”. In his book “The Jesus I Never Knew”, author Philip Yancey said: “I have learned that faith means trusting in advance what will only make sense in reverse.” Yancey said that God gave us the freedom to believe in him or not. Relying again on faith, not on evidence. The question remains unanswered, i.e. what does this God want to happen?

In conclusion:

In these three attempted answers in the preceding paragraph, humans are basically asked to have faith and believe in God because we cannot really know, or understand, what He is up to. This seems not good enough by a long stretch. Why, if there is a God, has He allowed very serious doubts to arise about His existence because of the large number of problematic issues with the Bible, among other difficulties. Added to this, he has failed to give any clear evidence of his existence over the whole of Modern Humans 300,000 year sojourn on planet Earth.

Humans evolved from a series of upright bipeds. At each stage of the bipedal evolution one major change was the increase in brain size. If a God was in charge of this evolutionary process He ensured we, MH had bigger, better brains than our predecessors in the Homo line. In which case surely this means He wants us to be able to clearly work out the origin of everything? If He wants us to have ‘freewill’ in deciding whether or not to believe in Him it’s reasonable to think that He must give more than hints, which are so weak they can be shot down in flames. He must also make things clear to all MH, from our very outset, 300,000 years ago. He has not done this, so the only reasonable assessment is to conclude there is no indisputable evidence of His existence. No “Yes” no “No” then no God.
