Chapter 7

Out of Africa

Pages 54–60

Where did we all come from? Yes, I know you know, but aside from the womb, or even maternalistic storks, where did Homo Sapiens, sometimes called Modern Humans (MH) originate? The hot money, and even the slightly warm dosh, is on Africa. This is known as the single origin, out of Africa theory. It says that all Modern Humans originate from Africa. This is backed up by impressive analysis of mitochondrial DNA. The genetic evidence strongly suggests we all, apart from some Africans, have the same ancestral lineage, with an Africa origin.

A much less supported alternate theory of MH origin is the multi-regional model, which has a few variants. This model proposes that Homo Sapiens arose from evolutionary ancestors, possibly Homo Heidelbergensis or Homo Erectus, in several locations across the globe at about the same time. The multi-regional theory is now very much on the wane. Africa and the single origin is almost unassailed but not yet absolutely so.

If mankind’s story started in Africa what evidence is there of very early human existence in that continent?

It follows that archaeological discoveries in Africa of MH ought to be the oldest found on planet Earth. Holding the prize for nearly 40 years, as the location with the oldest finds of MH, was at Omo, Ethiopia. Here, in 1967, Richard Leakey uncovered evidence of Modern Humans. His find revealed MH existence of about 200,000 years ago. But in 2004 fresh discoveries at Jebel Irhoud, Morocco were made by Jean-Jacques Hublin.

p.55

Originally, the Jebel Irhoud remains were thought to be Neanderthal but since it’s been suggested they are in fact MH bones, 300,000 years old. If confirmed, these will be the oldest remains of MH yet discovered (as at 2020). If we do originate from Morocco, that means we arrived after 99.8% of the cosmos’ existence. We are new kids on the block. Similar types of bones, now also thought likely to be of MH, have been found in Florisbad, South Africa. The Florisbad bones are dated to 260,000 years old. Archaeologists have not found traces of MH existence on any other continent dating this far back. It’s all about Africa for many millennia.

Before going on further, a little bit of translation might be useful for those of us who are not Latin scholars. Modern Humans (MH) are technically known as Homo Sapiens. From the Latin, Homo means man and Sapiens wise. Despite we can all quote historical and personal evidence to the contrary, for the entirety of their existence on planet Earth, all MH are wise men.

What do we know about these wise men, the ancient Moroccan and other Africans. They were the earliest of our modern human ancestors. Not much to say about them, because they were few of them, so there has not been a great deal to find. There were about 10,000 MH in Africa, before the gradual dispersal to the rest of the world, possibly through a main migration 60,000 to 70,000 years ago. So, in the millennia before the dispersal to other continents there would have been far fewer than 10,000. Climate and geography were also not conducive to preserving archaeological clues of these very early MH. Finally, they lived in a pre-historic period which means we have no written evidence to help us.

One of the few ancient African artifacts that we know of are the pierced bead shells that were discovered at the Grotte des Pigeons, Taforalt in Morocco. The shells are dated 82,000 years ago. The Aterian, North African, culture they represent is now thought to be from at least 110,000 years past. Previous to the Taforalt find, the Blombos caves, in South Africa were in the lead as the earliest evidence of Modern Human behaviour. Here similar shell beads were dated to 72,000 years ago. Blombos also shows evidence of the use of ochre, a red colouring agent. This has no useful purpose and can only be used for ornamentation. Also discovered was some abstract art.

p.56

The Blombos ochre was found in layers dated from 70,000 to 100,000 years ago. Apart from a centre of art, we know that Blombos was also a fishing base. As might be expected, with such a thin veneer of early Africans there is even less knowledge of any spiritual or religious activity. One lead though is the possible traces of python worship 70,000 years ago in the Ngamiland region of Botswana. The San people in this area of the Kalahari traditionally believe that mankind originated from the python. Twisting ancient, dry stream beds that ring the hills are said to have been made by the snake as it circled, forever looking for water.

Heading for pastures new

It appears that MH began to populate most of the rest of the world; from Africa about 70,000 years ago. 90% of modern-day non-Africans inherit DNA from this small group. But in 2018 finds in India, if confirmed, suggest a much earlier dispersal, at least 250,000 years ago, however it is not certain these were modern humans. They may be MH precursors.

There is growing possibility of several breakouts from Africa. The Bab el Mandeb one, 70,000 years back, may just be the most successful. New findings are always likely to alter the planetary dispersal date. In January 2018, scientists at Wales, Aberystwyth University published a paper on climatic conditions in Ethiopia going back 150,000 years. This suggested that conditions were favourable to allow humans to move out to the rest of the world much earlier, up to 130,000 years ago, then there’s the Indian possibility mentioned in the previous paragraph.

There may have been some movement out of Africa by both Northern, via Levant (Sinai, Israel and Syria) and Southern routes. But to date, the best evidence of a planetary-wide, permanent move is that a small tribe crossed over from that Southern route, via the southern end of the Red sea, 70,000 years ago. The crossing point is known as the Bab el Mandeb straits. They landed on the Arabian side probably somewhere near what is now Yemen or Aden. How they achieved the crossing is not known. Perhaps they used coracle type small boats, or rafts.

We know that both modern humans and Neanderthals used rafts. There is evidence in the Mediterranean at Cova de sa Multa of Neanderthal rafts back to 300,000 BP. Rafts may even have been used by Homo Sapiens ancestor, Homo Erectus, as long as 800,000 years ago.

The pioneering breakout from Africa would not have been as arduous seventy thousand years ago as it would be now. During that time sea levels in the region were much lower than they are today. The distance across the Red Sea that our Stone Age ancestors had to travel would have been much shorter than the same journey today.

Even so, this must have been a huge undertaking for our ancestors. Why did they attempt it? We can only speculate. It’s unlikely that the continental shift of home ground was for a tribal holiday. This dangerous journey into the unknown probably stemmed from the pressure simply to survive and continue to exist. Maybe the pioneering tribe was running short of food on the African side. Expanding human population may have resulted in competition for limited food resources. The coast line facing them across the narrow straits would be clearly visible and it would be tempting to go over. It is a common Human trait to believe, or want to believe, that “the grass is greener on the other side.” A mixture of hunger; the threat from strangers; and simple hope of a better life are most likely to be what drove them to attempt the perilous crossing. But they did attempt it and achieve it. We can be pretty sure of that.

From that Aden crossing Modern Humans went on to colonise the whole landed world, give or take the odd desert, icy waste and mountain extremity.

Eve, the Earth mother

Interestingly, all non-sub-Saharan Modern Humans have a common ancestor. Modern DNA research tell us that we are all related to just one woman who came out of Africa with the first tribe to shift continents. She is known as Mitochondrial Eve. Technically she is the most recent common matrilineal ancestor. This does not do Eve any justice. She should be given the title of Earth mother.

Only sub-Saharan Africans and 10% of everyone else may not have Eve’s DNA. If it were not for Eve, most of us might not exist.

It’s astonishing to think that the billions of people alive today, of all colours and sizes are related. We all go back to Eve, the Earth Mother. This includes people as diverse and generally small, narrow eyed Japanese to tall blond-haired Scandinavians, from dark skinned and dark eyed Aborigines to red haired and green-eyed Scots. All have this common, ancient ancestor. She was a woman whose gene line had the good fortune to survive through four thousand generations. Good on you Eve, we all owe our lives to you. You deserve a statue, a Nobel prize, commemorative coinage and a city named after you.

The voyage across the southern end of the Red Sea was the Stone-agers second attempt to move out of Africa. The first try was several hundred miles to the north of the successful Aden traverse. Favourable climatic conditions, which resulted in a green Sahara, led to this earlier settlement in the Near East at caves in Skhul and Qafzeh, Israel. These caves show signs of modern human occupation between 90,000 and 120,000 years ago. Unlike the later attempt at Bab el Mandeb this was not the sling-shot that launched modern humans on their path to world domination, although a small group might have got to India. The barrier imposed by the terrain of that time probably largely restricted further movement eastwards. It was mainly desert, Arabian and Sinai, and mountain ranges. This would have posed huge problems. The lack of ambulatory success by these ancient ancestors is given credence by the dating of the earliest known MHs in Europe, from a site in Bulgaria, the Bacho Kiro Cave, to around 45,000 years ago. It does not take 55,000 or so years to walk from Israel to Bulgaria, even if the roads were very bad (more accurately, non-existent).

The Aden arrivals, crossed the water around 20,000 years after the Skhul lot. Their onward migration probably followed coastal paths. The ocean is always a potential source of food, and, for the most part, travelling might be more easily conducted at sea level, near to the sea.

Fair dinkum walkabout

Curiously, evidence suggests MHs reached Australia seemingly before setting foot in continental Europe. We know from finds in Kakadu, 150 kilometres from Darwin, Australia, that the first Aussies were enjoying a barbie on the beach about 65,000 years ago. This is when a small group of MH landed in northern Australia, maybe using a raft to cross 90 kilometres of sea.

From Africa to Australia, without any form of mechanical transport, wow what a trek, no wonder it took those ancient people around 5,000 years. But modern Europeans were not in evidence until about 40,000 years ago, a minimum of 26,000 years after Aussies were chasing Wallabies. Perhaps the tendency to follow coastlines accounts for this dichotomy. It looks like the ground-breaking route to Europe was via India.

Those original Europeans were known as Cro-Magnon people, named after the big cave near Les Eyzies-de-Tayac-Sireuil in southwest France. Here, remains of these ancient people were discovered.

Today terminology has moved on, instead of Cro-Magnon, the earliest Europeans are known, rather unoriginally, as European Early Modern Humans (or EEMH).

Humans took with them their hunting skills, spirituality and art on their travels. These skills and beliefs developed along similar but slightly different paths due to isolation; lack of communication between groups; and time periods (thousands of years). Australian aborigines inherited a spiritual culture handed down verbally generation to generation right up to the present day. Aborigines are renowned for their unique belief in Dreaming, where every person is thought to exist for ever in Dream Time. Ancient aborigines also used an original hunting weapon, the boomerang. They did a bit of art work too. The oldest authenticated Aboriginal paintings have been discovered at Ubirr, in Arnhem Land, Northern Australia. These date from 30,000 BCE.

The last parts of the world to be colonized were North and South America. Human big game hunters reached North America from what is now Russia across the Bering Plain to Alaska. Sea levels were lower then.

Not surprisingly, artistic evidence from the Americas is of more recent origin than elsewhere in the world.

Californian petroglyphs (rock etchings) at Bishop speculatively might be 20,000 years old, but Mexico holds the oldest evidence of human occupation in N and S America. The Chiquihuite Cave, in Mexico, shows humans were there about 30,000 years ago.

Toba coughs up

p.60

Peopling of the planet and expansion of population numbers did not go unhindered. A major natural catastrophe occurred about 74,000 years ago. The super volcano that was Toba, in what is now Sumatra, let rip in a very big way. The blast was so big it affected the whole planet. The better-known Krakatoa eruption of 1883 was the equivalent of a largish firework going off compared to mighty Toba’s sizeable bomb.

The ancient eruption spewed many tens of millions of tons of dust and gas high into the atmosphere. This was carried by weather systems around the globe. The sun was partially blocked for 6 to 8 years by all the debris from Toba, resulting in a volcanic winter with temperatures worldwide falling between 3 to 5 degrees. Global cooling lasted for 1,000 years. Not surprisingly there was a huge detrimental impact on the animals and plants that humans hunted and gathered. The result of all this was a dramatic fall in Modern Human population numbers, they dropped from an estimated 100,000 to below 15,000.

Ever since Toba MH population numbers have generally been on an upward trend, despite the mitigating effect of Malthusian war, famine and pestilence.

We are now over 8 billion and still rising, at least until another major extinction event like Toba, or the comet that did for the dinosaurs. Maybe Climate Change will be a major brake on population numbers too.

Some gloomy pundits say Climate Change will result in massive population movements because of rising sea levels. It will also cause water and food shortages, perhaps starvation, particularly in poorer countries. Other than a big volcano or comet; Climate Change, wars, pandemics, like COVID 19, may act as a severe brake on population size in mankind’s future. One or more of these will possibly result in a Malthusian catastrophe.
