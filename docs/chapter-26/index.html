Chapter 26

Humans see the Past and Travel so Fast

Pages 455–458

Some good pub quiz questions could be these:

  1. How long have humans been in the Universe?
  2. Can humans go back in time?
  3. What is the fastest speed that any human has travelled?
  4. For a bonus point, have any bits of us moved faster than answer to 3? 3

All the answers are below.

We were there at The Beginning (almost!) and will be there at The End

Our universe cannot be avoided. It’s all around us. We are a part of it. An infinitesimal, tiny fragment, but still a part, and we will always be a part of it. In fact, we’ve been in this universe of ours almost since its beginning, 13.8 billion years ago. Shortly after the kick-off - that Big Bang - atoms began to be formed. The first atoms were of hydrogen, appearing about 380,000 years after the BB. Our bodies are made up of atoms, molecules and their basic components - electrons and quarks. As previously noted, the great Carl Sagan said “We are star stuff.” These fundamental particles of ours are pretty ancient and will go on for a long, long time after our demise. The particles that make up our bodies are as old as 99.99% of the universe’s age. We will be there at the end, nearly, but in an unconscious, unstructured, very bitty form.

Looking up at the Time Machine

An interesting point to note, among many, is that when we look out at the night sky with our eyes, binoculars or telescopes we are always looking back in time. The nearest star to our solar system, Proxima Centauri, is 4.2 billion light years away. That means we see it as it was just over 4 years ago. Distant galaxies, spied with the mighty Hubble space telescope, are many billions of light years away. The light we see from some galaxies originated before planet Earth was formed, 4.6 billion years ago. This feature gives rise to another cosmological term, the Cosmic Event Horizon named for the edge of the universe. Any light emitted today from the CEH can never reach us, because the space in between is moving away faster than the speed of light. The Cosmic Event Horizon is 16 billion light years away.

Eventually, all galaxies and stars beyond the CEH won’t be seen. There will just be black, empty looking space. Of course, by the time this happens humans probably won’t be around to see it, or rather not see it

So Still yet so Fast and so Far

Perception is not truth. It is all in the eye of the beholder and what is commonly accepted. (see philosophers Xenophanes, Bishop Berkeley, and Michel Foucault).

We think that we can be sure about something, but that depends on the angle from which we are looking. Apparent certainties can be just that - apparent. For instance, when someone has a good night’s sleep, say sleeping for 8 hours that person might say that they had not moved any distance in the night. But the Earth revolves completely once every 24 hours. Meaning Earth is turning on its axis at a rate of just over 1,000 miles an hour.

The Earth is not only turning on its axis. It also orbits our Sun. Earth orbits the Sun in 1 year, covering 150 million miles at a speed of 67,000 miles an hour. The Sun is moving in relation to the Milky Way galaxy, dragging Earth and other solar system planets with it at a respectable 486,000 miles an hour. Lastly, our Milky Way galaxy itself is moving through the universe, along with its 200 billion stars, including the Sun and planet Earth. Our galaxy moves through space at a rate of 1,342,000 miles an hour.

The sleeper, is unaware that he’s moved in a number of directions, at different speeds, during his 8 hours sleep.

To recap:

  • Our sleeper has rotated with the Earth at a measly 1,000 miles an hour, shifting position some 8,000 miles;The Earth speeds around the Sun at 67,000 miles an hour. He’s moved with it; a distance of 536,000 miles;
  • Our Sun races around its home galaxy at 486,000 mph. Now our sleeper has moved 3,888,000 miles;
  • The Sun itself is hauled along with the Milky Way’s part in universal expansion. In 8 hours, with our sleeper on board, moving at 1.3 million mph, it has travelled 10,736,000 miles.

So, during his night’s good sleep our slumberer has travelled a maximum distance equivalent to taking 20 return trips to the moon and back.

But he thinks he hasn’t moved all night, apart from turning over occasionally, or going to the loo perhaps. Quite a moving story don’t you think?

Finally, on speed, bits of us, that is tiny, non-sentient, fundamental particles have moved fastest of all. These have speeded along at near the speed of light when they were originally ejected from a supernova - we are star stuff remember.

Some very moving images:

Planet Earth and all its inhabitants rotate on its axis at over 1,000 mph

Something very big smacked into the Earth a long time ago. This caused Earth’s axis to tilt about 24° in relation to the sun, giving us the seasons.

The Earth, and us, orbit the Sun in 365 and a bit days at 67,000 mph

The Milky Way spiral galaxy speeds through space at 1.3 million mph, this is the fastest speed any and complete humans have travelled at. It’s continually speeding up, because of the universe’s expansion.

Expansion of space - impact on what can be seen from Earth

The Sun and its attendant planets, Earth included, spins in the Orion spiral arm of the Milky Way galaxy at 486,000 mph. We are at present 28,000 light years from the centre of the Milky Way. This distance is constantly increasing, because of the expansion of the universe. Eventually, after some billions of years, a great deal of the sky will be dark, when seen through a telescope. But, to the naked eye, it won’t look much different, because most of the stars we see now are in the Milky Way, and they will not move away. Local stars will still be visible, because they are gravitationally bound. This means stars in the Milky Way, Andromeda and about 60 nearby, smaller satellite galaxies, including the Small and Large Magellanic Clouds, will still be visible. Of course, this assumes there is some intelligent being to do the seeing.
