Chapter 1

First of all

Pages 6–11

In the beginning, there was a flash and a BANG, or perhaps first a bang and then a flash. The laws of physics had not yet been invented so sound might have sped past light, before light established its relative supremacy. The bang was a very big bang; the most gigantic bang there has ever been. Imagine an indescribably decibelic loudness. It was that big.

Well, that’s what you might surmise had happened if you’ve heard of the Big Bang theory without studying it more closely. You would be excused for thinking that an enormous explosion took place, excitingly flinging the stuff of galaxies, stars and planets in all directions - the Big Bang. Disappointingly there was no bang. Those kill-joy cosmologists say so, despite this they still call it, confusingly, the Big Bang.

Many of these scientists say that at the very beginning there was just an infinitesimally small, dense and hot object - something far smaller than an atom, impenetrably dense and much hotter than any star. This object, a singularity to be precise, started to expand. In fact, it did blow up, but like a balloon rather than a bomb. So, our universe began to ever increase by rapid, incredibly rapid, inflation; even faster than the speed of light. It was also a silent event. No one heard any puffing, despite the blowing up. No one saw anything either. There was no one to hear or see, except maybe Him. If there was a Him at this singular moment in time, some 13.8 billion years ago.

That is one account of universal origin. It has widespread support in the scientific community. But some religiously vectored people claim it is an invention of the godless and not the true explanation. These Creationists think the whole process started just 6,000 years ago and was both speedier and simpler. “Let there be light and there was light.” They say that He said that.

HE followed on with a week’s hard work. Unpaid too. There were no unions then. On the penultimate morn of His evolutionary week Adam burst forth. Eventually that day there was Eve. Did the universe start like this?

He wrote of the Great A’Tuin, a 10,000 kilometers long space turtle, rendezvousing with its own kind in the depths of space. The different sex space-turtles engaged in an almighty big bang, with procreation firmly in mind. Well, if not in mind exactly, somewhere else quite vital in space-turtle reproductive anatomy. A thing so big, it’s only detectable with astronomical rather than anatomical instruments.

Setting aside space-turtles we have two beginnings in the frame, the Big Bang and Creationism theories. There are many others. But one beginning is enough. Two beginnings are more than enough. That is, if you are mathematically inclined. Philosophers would argue the point. Theologians wouldn’t. They have faith in their certainty and certainty in their faith. Artists can picture any beginning, so they trump everyone else.

But the beginning is only the start of what we are about here. Was there a Him? Was He then and is He now and will He be forever and forever? That is the great God question we will explore. It’s the question above all questions. An easy question to ask. The hardest to answer. A question that has intrigued mankind’s greatest minds over thousands of years.

Let’s look at what some of these sages thought about a God, through their expertise with philosophy, religion and science, particularly palaeontology, history, cosmology and quantum mechanics. Also explored is the influence of environment, human nature and human desires on the God issue.

How to address these very far-reaching subject areas? Well, broadly, this book attempts a topic based, chronological approach. Exploring the Goddish history alongside the history of humans, Earth and the cosmos seems to be a reasonable method. Humanity’s ingenuity has spawned many Gods and quite a few Goddesses too. And we must not forget the spirits and other supernatural entities. We can’t possibly cover them all in any reasonable depth. Our torch will therefore shine most brightly on the one God, He of Christianity; Islam and Judaism. So, let’s get going. We shall start, not with a bang or anything flash; we shall simply begin with the next paragraph.

But, really, don’t believe such a belief. For now, why not indulge your curiosity genes and read on? You can chuck it in the bin later.

Before exploring whether or not such a being as God exists we need to know more precisely what we are talking about when we refer to “God”. As already indicated, the principle, but not exclusive focus will be on the why and what of the monotheistic God, that is God in the singular; the all singing and all dancing God; chiefly, the Abrahamic one.

Of course, there have been and still are many religions and cultures where more than one God, or a supreme teacher, is thought to exist, but Godly monotheism has come to pre-dominate religious belief systems in modern times. He’s the one and He’s the only, not for the atheists though. The agnostics are not sure. Others have other beliefs.

The existence, or not, of a God

Many humans would agree that the existence of a God cannot be proved by rigorous scientific method. Despite this, the one God (Abrahamic) exists today for billions of people. But there is also a large and growing disbelief, many millions either deny or ignore His existence.

Gods, in one guise or another, have been around for the greater part of the 300 millennia that Homo Sapiens has inhabited planet Earth. First, Spirits associated with the natural world were a fundamental part of life for tens of thousands of years of early human history. As human societies developed religious beliefs grew too. They became more contrived, complex and competitive. The Spirits and animism of pre-historic, hunter gatherers evolved into a changing variety of religions and a huge pantheon of Gods.

HE, the one God, arguably arrived relatively late on the scene, mainly, but not solely, from biblical times up to the modern day. Over this later period the concept of a single, omniscient, omnipotent, pre-eminent being, generally came to dominate religious doctrine, although by no means exclusively so.

Spirits, Gods and religions will be explored in more detail later, especially monotheism, but first up it will be useful to try and understand why many modern, most historic and pre-historic humans, back to 300,000 years ago, needed or wanted some sort of God.
