Ice ages, the five major extinctions and terminology explained

Now for a short break from consideration of our early MH ancestors. You can skip the next 5 or 6 pages if you are familiar with the terminology used to describe the ancient world, or if you are not interested. Instead of explaining some technical wording in an appendix it’s coming up now, in the main body of the text. A few bits of necessary terminology have crept into this chapter, and the previous. Anyone reading around the topics covered may come across other relevant terminology too. It’s useful to set out some explanation of these and expand them. The reader can then form a clearer picture of the context. Since that reader is likely to me, I would find this of benefit as my ageing memory becomes less reliable.

Paleontology, Archaeology and Anthropology?

What’s a paleontologist, or with an extra ‘a’ palaeontologist, and how does this differ from an archaeologist? Well, the palaeontologist studies prehistoric fossils. These include animals, which could be humans, and plants. Generally, palaeontology is associated with fossils that are hundreds of thousands or more likely millions of years old. Palaeontologists are interested in all life; from bacteria to dinosaurs and beyond.

Archaeology, on the other hand, has a much narrower focus. It concentrates on the study of past humans; particularly their artifacts, such as tools and weapons, but they are also interested in human fossils. Since their focus is on humans, who haven’t been around that long, archaeologists are generally looking at shorter time periods, normally hundreds of thousands of years, that is if the subject is a modern human or a Neanderthal. To slightly confuse matters further, there are also specialists called anthropologists. These are focused on both past and present human behaviour, language and culture. The three specialists have different primary interests but they do overlap a little. All three disciplines study clues from the past, to gain a fuller picture of life on this planet.

Geological Epochs and Periods

Apart from the fossil related sciences of palaeontology, archaeology and anthropology there are definitions for various geological timescales to consider. The most commonly used geological time definitions are: Eras; Periods and Epochs.

  • Eras embrace a timescale of 100 million years or more;
  • Periods tens of million years;
  • Epochs just a few million, or shorter.

The various classifications define important events that have occurred over the course of Earth’s history, like mass extinctions. There are estimated to have been at least five mass extinction events over the last 540 million years. Our present world emerges from the change engendered by these cataclysmic events of the past.

The best-known example is the asteroid that struck in the Gulf of Mexico 65 MYA. This ended the 165 million years reign of dinosaurs (excluding birds) and accounted for 83% of reptiles. Three-quarters of all plants and animals died in a relatively short time. This is known as the Cretaceous-Tertiary period mass extinction.

And what Era, Period and Epoch do we humans live in? Modern Humans, i.e. Homo Sapiens, have been around for 200,000 years. We have existed in an Era of which most of us are ignorant, the Cenozoic. This stretches back 65 million years and marks the boundary with the last of the terrestrial dinosaurs. The Cenozoic Era is split into three periods. MH and their hominid ancestors fall mainly within the last one, the Quaternary period. These periods are further reduced into Epochs. The Quaternary is composed of into two Epochs: Pleistocene and Holocene.

As of now we MH live in the:

  • Cenozoic Era (the last 65 million years);
  • Quaternary Period (the last 2.6 million years);
  • Holocene Epoch (11.7 thousand years, up to today)

Some experts claim that the arrival of industrial age humans has resulted in such a massive impact on the planet that we are now in the Anthropocene Epoch. However, this designation had not yet been approved by the International Union of Geological Sciences as at May 2020 but there is growing pressure to formally recognize the Anthropocene, as commencing from the mid twentieth century. The Pleistocene Epoch, which preceded the current Holocene Epoch, covered a period of 2.6 million to about 12,000

p.63

years ago. This spanned the period of Earth’s most recent glaciations and marked the end of the Paleolithic age.

The first 2.4 million years of the Pleistocene saw the continued rise of our evolutionary predecessors, the bipedal ape derivatives. In fact, the earliest hominids, like Australopithecus and Ardipithecus Ramidus, walked upright for the first time in an even early epoch than the Pleistocene. The two A’s were on their feet about 4.4 million years ago, during the Pliocene epoch. And Ardi R’s ancestor, Ardi Kamada, was around even earlier, 5.6 MYA. So, hominids covered three Epochs. But Modern Humans occupy just two, discounting for the moment the Anthropocene.

Holocene: from ancient Greek holos = entire and kainos = new, the whole term means entirely recent. It pre-dates Egyptian, written - hieroglyphic - history.

MH were hunter-gatherers during the time of ice (mostly), that is the Pleistocene Epoch. They survived into the current epoch, the Holocene, when written history began, with Sumerian cuneiform writing on stone, using wedge shapes. Later the Egyptians used a pictorial, hieroglyphic language.

Shamans

There is one specific human profession that has spanned both the Pleistocene and Holocene Epochs. No, not the female one commonly thought of as the “oldest profession on earth”. The job I refer to was seen, in many human communities, as a top drawer rather than a drop drawers’ occupation - the shaman.

Shamanism still exists today in some indigenous communities, as it has since MH were first about, 300,000 years ago. It is the oldest spiritual or religious practice in the world.

What are shamans? They are expert, and sometimes unique, human intermediaries with spirits. Shamans believe they are a conduit to the spirit world.

p.64

Commonly, they follow specific rituals, chant certain mantras and dress in a distinctive fashion. Sometimes, with aid of repetitive movement, chanting and music the shaman enters a trance state in his quest to enter the spirit world. They seek to heal sick people by driving out evil spirits and can perform rituals to help with hunting game animals. Shamans are strongly associated with the natural world. Not only ritual but also music often formed a part of the professional shaman’s modus operandi. Depending on the culture, this can involve drums; rattles or gongs. Feathers, smoking pipes or swords can play an important role in shamanistic ceremony. Significantly in shamanism there is no sign of the one God.

Ice Ages, Glacial Periods, Stadials and Inter-stadials

Sometimes when ice ages are mentioned it’s not at all clear when they happened and what areas of our planet were affected. Then expressions like stadials and inter-stadials can creep in.

Let’s start with ice ages. These are lengthy cool periods which can last millions of years. Why does an ice age occur? There are four reasons:

  • The greatest likely influence is the change in Earth’s orbit around the sun and even the sun’s orbit around the galaxy. The earth’s position relative to the sun changes over time, causing a variation in the amount of sunlight we receive. These Earth orbital changes are known as the Milankovitch cycles.

  • Changes in CO₂ (carbon dioxide or greenhouse gas) concentration has occurred over time. In the past this may have come about by the impact of large meteorites or wide scale volcanic activity. Carbon dioxide can also result from plants and animals. Carbon dioxide can act like a blanket, with too much of it we get global warming, as at present. The growing impact humans have had is human-caused, but it has a huge impact on the environment. If there is much less carbon dioxide, this leads to cooling and ice formation. Significantly decreased levels of CO₂ can trigger an ice age.

  • Continental drift, which affects the location of continents and oceans and can impact on the total heat absorbed from the sun;

  • The rise of large mountain ranges, like the Andes and Himalayas, can impact on air circulation and cause warming or cooling.

The average planetary surface temperature falls sufficiently during an ice age to allow vast sheets of ice and enormous glaciers to form. These covered much of the Earth’s surface. The last ice age started about 2.5 million years ago. At its peak, known as a glacial maximum, much of what are now temperate regions were iced up. This included much of Germany, Poland, Russia and northern Britain. 20,000 years ago, the glacial ice sheet of North America covered land below the great lakes. It’s generally known that most of human pre-history happened during an ice age.

We know about woolly mammoths roaming the frozen tundra. They have been depicted by many artists and they’ve been dug up out of Siberian permafrost often in a well-preserved state. These giant, shaggy beasts were a favourite prey of our early human ancestors.

Woolly Mammoths, creatures of the old ice (Pleistocene) They survived for 400,000 years, up to about 4,000 years ago.

From within an Ice Age there can be a period called a Glacial. Glacials are colder, shorter periods during an existing ice age. They are thought to last about 12,000 years but there is some evidence showing they could last up to 28,000 years. Conversely an Interglacial is a short period of warming during the millions of years of an ice age. There are two others, less common terms to get to grips with in the climate context: stadials and inter-stadials. A stadial is an even shorter cooler period during an interglacial. Yes, you’ve guessed it, an interstadial is a short warmer period during a glacial.

p.67

Let’s put these geological climatic periods into some perspective. Taking length measurement as an example, ice-ages are equivalent to measurement in kilometers; glacial and inter-glacial equivalent measure is in meters and stadial or inter-stadial measures in centimeters.

The five ice ages in the Earth’s history are sometimes called ice houses. These represent about 15% of Earth’s history with 85% in green house stages. The worst known ice age was so severe the Earth may have looked like a complete snowball. The ice reached the tropics and even oceans froze over. Fortunately, this did not bother any ancient MH or their immediate ancestors. There were none about.

Snowball Earth occurred between about 717 to 635 million years ago (the Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations), in pre-Cambrian times. This was a period before the dinosaurs and even before any vertebrate animals existed. Well, if they had existed they would have become extinct, unless they could thrive on a snow and ice diet. MH at this time were still a very long way off, way down the evolutionary production line.

Although, as mentioned earlier, currently the Earth is in an interglacial period called the Holocene some think differently. Ecologist, Eugene Stormer and chemist Paul J. Crutzen argue that we are now in a new era, the Anthropocene. This is an era when MHs are now so numerous and industrialised their activities are impacting on Earth’s climate and ecosystems. Hence the continuing concern over human induced climate change and extinction of living organisms. If we humans do become partly responsible for a mass extinction this will be the sixth time in Earth’s history. We can confidently say we’re not responsible for the other five.

p.68

That’s enough on geographical and climatic terminology except to remember that when there is a cold year during a period we suppose it will be warm this is because climate is what we expect, while weather is what we get (i.e. The former is long term, the latter short term, which can be an interstadial.).

Did He (God) cause these catastrophic events? Were they a part of a carefully constructed holy plan? No doubt there are devout, religious people who think this is so. Others will take the view from science that these extinction events are all explainable without the need for divine intervention. Some forms of life survived each extinction event, fortunately for us. Are we now leading into a 6th mass extinction event, as at 2021? Climate Change scientists say this is a possibility, partly caused by the huge contribution MH have made to greenhouse gases, like CO₂ and methane.

There’s been a meteoric rise in these since the industrial revolution, contributing to global warming, but as we see from Earth’s history other periods of global warming occurred, unaided by humans. Humans were completely unaffected by these earlier periods of global warming, that’s because they had not yet evolved during these sweaty times, a minimum of 65 million years ago.

After early planetary history, the biggest temperature rise was across the Permian - Triassic boundary, about 250 million years ago. At this time the average global temperature exceeded 22°c, with sea surface temperatures at the equator in excess of 40°c. 95% of all planetary species were killed off. Hopefully, Earth, through human assisted Anthropocene Climate Change, is not on the way to breaking this record. If it does succeed, there may not be many humans left to log the feat.
