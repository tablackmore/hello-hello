Chapter 14

Europe: emerging civilisation and religion

Pages 116–119

We’ve been bouncing around the world, tracing the development of various early civilisations and assorted religious beliefs. Europeans generally could not claim to be among the front-runners of civilisations. Most of theirs came a bit later than those speedsters of Mesopotamia, Egypt, India and China. But they did spawn one developed society which could compete in terms of ancientness. This was the Minoan Civilisation.

Minoans had their base on the large island of Crete. The name comes from a legendary King, Minos. They flourished on Crete for about 1,200 years, from 2600 to 1400 BCE, maybe a bit later. Their’s was a written language, so complicated and original in its construction that it is yet to be deciphered. Minoans were great builders. Paved roads connected cities and streets had water and sewage systems. The palace at Knossos was up to five stories high with staircases, water pipes, a drainage system and beautiful friezes. There were other palaces, indicating monarchial type governance. Their buildings, with columns, exhibited some evidence of Egyptian influence. Not surprising given Egypt is only 600 kilometres away across the Mediterranean Sea, that’s nothing to serious seafarers. Minoan society was unusual in that it was matriarchal. Descent was traced through the female line. This might explain why their main deity was probably a Sun Goddess. They had other Goddesses, specializing in fertility, harvest, household, animals, snakes and the underworld. There were a few male Godly varieties too. Religious practices are thought to have involved dance, procession and sacrifice.

p.117

It’s thought some of the Minoan Goddesses and religious ideas may have been a base for the religion of ancient Greece.

A famous Minoan myth is of the Minotaur. This monstrous, half bull-like creature had an unusual food preference. His favorite diet consisted of young maidens and youths. These he was provided with in fear, as a placatory tribute. He gobbled up the teenagers every 9 months or so in his underground Cretan maze called the labyrinth. The Minotaur was eventually slain by the hero Theseus. Local young maidens and youths no longer had to worry about becoming his dinner. You may think this story is a load of bull rather than half of one.

The cause for the quite sudden demise of the Minoan civilisation is not clear. It could have resulted either from an erupting volcano, a major earthquake or invading Mycenaean armies from mainland Greece. Perhaps it was two or all these.

Like the Minoans with their Minotaur, Mycenaeans had their own well-known heroic tale. Homer in his Iliad helped them out here. Written in the 8th century BCE this epic poem describes the great King Agamemnon, his Trojan war, dispute with Achilles, and the ten-year siege of Troy. The story gets a bit mixed, several writers put their oar in. In the fondest known version there was that wooden horse. The Trojans must have been a bit wooden too, bringing it inside their impregnable fortress then suffering terribly from those horse droppings. All that fuss, because of the most beautiful woman in the world. The face that launched a thousand ships, and several films, caused a lot of trouble. That Helen, she was too pretty by half.

p.118

Just as the end of the Minoans is shrouded in mystery we have no clear explanation for the demise of the Mycenaeans. One hypothesis is that it was caused by internal strife. What we are sure of is that the Mycenaeans led the way to classical Greece.

Other ancient Mediterranean civilisations

Before we move on to classical Greece two other conglomerate peoples need a brief mention. These two are the Phoenicians and Etruscans. They could be considered to be in the second division of civilisations, because of their smaller size and lack of lasting impact.

Phoenicians were merchant traders in cities bordering the Mediterranean along the coast of modern-day Lebanon, Syria and bits of Turkey - the Levant. The cities acted independently but formed a league. It’s not clear whether they considered themselves a single entity. Their name Phoenicia refers to the purple cloth from this region. Phoenicia lasted from about 1500 BCE until Cyrus the Great, of Persia, trampled over them in 539 BCE. The period of 400 years between 1200 to 800 BCE was their purple patch. They flourished then because of the decline of the neighboring, mighty Hittite and Egyptian Empires. Their customs and religion did not markedly differ from other parts of the Levant. Perhaps Phoenicia’s lasting claim to fame is because they founded the iconic city of Carthage, a major city of the Roman Empire. An important Goddess was Astarte. She had a wide brief as Goddess of war, sexuality and fertility. Also known as Astoreth and Ishtar, she was important in other cultures - particularly Canaan, Assyrian, Babylonian and Egyptian.

p.119

The Etruscans were wealthy traders located at a different part of the Mediterranean than Minoans, Mycenaeans and Phoenicians. Their home base was in Italy, on the western coast, covering much of Tuscany. They lasted a relatively short period, from 800 BCE until gradually swallowed up by the Romans after 509 BCE.

Their architecture was influenced by ancient Greece but Etruscan religion showed originality, especially early on. According to the Roman historian Livy, Etruscans were particularly religious. They considered the Gods had an impact on every aspect of their lives. It was important therefore to consult them and try to find out what may be going to happen through study of animal entrails, and the liver. They looked for guidance in the movement of birds, clouds and the stars.

Top God was Tinia, Zeus and Jupiter’s equivalent. There were at least 40 other Gods, including female divinities like Turan. She corresponded to the Greek Aphrodite, Goddess of love and beauty. Etruscans believed there was a real underworld where the dead made their way to. Etruscans were finished when defeated by the army of the Roman Consul Valerius in 509 BCE.

Next up are the two early European civilisations who have really left a big and lasting impact, the Greeks and Romans.
