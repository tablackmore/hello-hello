Eternity

Eternity lasts for quite a long time. God’s there throughout. Does He get bored?

Here we have, many claim, not just a supreme being but THE Supreme Being. This Supreme Being, in His traditional monotheistic God mode, knows everything; can go anywhere and anywhen. He is all powerful and exists for ever. At least, that is what most of His adherents claim of Him.

How does such a being retain interest in continued existence? Nothing is impossible to Him. His capabilities can’t be stretched. No one is in his league. He is incapable of forgetting anything. God knows what’s going to happen before it happens. For Him existence just goes on and on and on, forever and ever. Looking at the Trinity, three as the one, means He does not have family, nor does He have friends. No one is on His level so it’s impossible for Him to have interesting discussions with anyone. He, apparently, doesn’t do jokes or laughter, so there is no refuge in humour for this Abrahamic God. What does He do for recreation? He has many universes for His playground, but how can He utilise these to relieve potential tedium?

We can investigate a little here. Let’s again employ the Tardis, our time travelling machine, to visit God in his heaven nearly 14 billion years ago. We find that He’s been thinking about what to do to pass the time. Our detectoring reveals that He is about to take His cue from the leisure activities of one of the beings He will create. i.e. God moves ahead 13.8 billion years on the space/time curve of one of his universes to take a quick look at what some humans eventually do at the weekends. Sadly, he notes, some even do this on Sundays.

God discusses his idea with someone close to Him. With the help of the Tardis, We are back in time to just before the Big Bang. We hear this conversation between God and his angel-in-chief Gabriel:

God: “Gabriel, I’ve taken a look into the future, I see that these humans I’m eventually going to bring into existence spend a lot of leisure time playing something called golf.” “What is golf Lord?” asks Gabriel. God: “Apparently golf is a healthy, outdoor activity with an opportunity for serious socialising on the 19th tee. The basic idea seems to be to knock a ball into a hole.”

“Yes Lord?” queried Gabriel, not quite seeing where this was heading. (as senior advisor Gabriel is allowed to forego the “My” which would normally precede the “Lord” when addressing God) God: “Well, I thought that I could turn a suitable galaxy into a golf course. Then you and I could have a game occasionally.” Gabriel: “Ah, I see, very good idea Lord. Of course, you are the Supreme Being with unlimited powers so no doubt you could easily construct an excellent golf-course. In fact, it would instantly become the best in all the universes.” (Gabriel had been God’s right-hand angel for countless billions of years. He knew when a bit of flattery was in order; even if he did not fully grasp the topic under discussion.)

“Yes, of course” God replied, completely unaware that he had made a very small joke.

God went on: “We could play with rounded, dimpled and extra dense asteroids and try potting them in a galaxy wide course of 18 black holes. Strategically placed Red Giant stars would act as bunkers.” “On second thoughts,” said God pausing, “Close up, black isn’t really my colour.” God hesitated a fraction and then continued: “I know, I’ll make them white holes instead of black holes.” Gabriel: “Lord, are you sure you want to be seen as preferring white to black? This will become a serious PC issue with later humans. They may eventually discover your golfing galaxy and be dismayed at your preference for white.”

“Nonsense” said God briskly, “there’s going to be a lot of black in the universe. Nearly all the stuff between the stars will be made of something called “dark matter”. That’s near as dammit black. No one should complain because the black holes in just the one galaxy are white.” God continued, “Come to think of it, I’m not keen on “Red” either. Bit of a hellish colour that. But I won’t make the bunkers white, in case the PC crowd get agitated, I’ll turn all the Red Giant stars (the intended bunkers) into Cerise Giants.” and in a triumphant tone God goes on: “Hardly anyone will know what colour cerise is so they won’t be able to accuse Me of colour discrimination.”

God and Gabriel would have a fairway to drive off on each hole of their galactic, star flung, course. Naturally, poor Gabriel would be on a hiding to nothing. God can achieve a hole in one with every shot, even on the 570 million light-year par 5.

Many, many years after setting up this course, God and Gabriel are in the celestial clubhouse bar.

p.187

“Nice round again Lord. That’s the 2 millionth and second time you’ve beaten me. As usual, you scored a hole in one at every hole.” Gabriel paused then continued: “You really are the holy one, aren’t you?” God, not having a sense of humour, but sensing that something is not quite right with Gabriel’s last remark replied: “Gabriel, am I missing something here, Did you attempt to make some sort of joke just then?” and, with a flash of slightly misplaced inspiration: “Do my socks need darning?” Look out on a clear night with a powerful telescope. Focus top left of the Milky Way, four galaxies on, you might just make out a Cerise Giant star or two and the white holes.

The Soul

Let’s examine this ephemeral, floaty, mystical essence through the five Ws analysis: what; why; where; when and who.

What is it?

The soul is thought to be a non-physical essence of self. It can exist after the body has died and is in effect immortal.

All the current major religions: Christianity; Islam and Judaism believe MH (Modern Humans) have a soul. There is quite a lot of theological discussion as to when the idea of a soul was introduced into Abrahamic religious belief. Some say the idea was borrowed from ancient Greek and other cultures. Plato, 2,400 year ago, thought MH had immortal souls.

For Christians, it was Origen, around 200 CE who first expounded the idea of a soul, claiming it was created in every human by God. The Hebrew word ‘nephesh’, in the Bible, is often translated to mean soul, but not consistently. The old religions (sometimes referred to by snooty monotheistic moderns as “pagan”) also believed there was some sort of spirit that allowed an existence after death, even if they did not name this spirit a soul. The soul cannot be seen, touched or in any other way detected. No bodily dissection, X ray, CT or ultrasound scan or any other type of medical examination has every produced evidence of the existence of a soul. It is only some religious people who suggest what a soul is and claim that it exists in every MH. There is not a shred of hard evidence for it, unless spiritual mediums or some people’s dreams are to be given that description.

Why claim there is one?

Why does the concept of a soul pervade nearly all religions? The existence of a soul, of sorts, is absolutely essential if any major religion is to have believers. The soul offers the engaging prospect of eternal life. And each religion, apart from some ancient ones, promises a very pleasant perpetual existence if only the believer keeps to certain rules during his/her time on planet Earth. There is no body, after death to enjoy the eternal life with, but the soul is a perfect vehicle to propel believers to heaven, nirvana or whatever endless delight is on offer. As an essential part of their sales pitch, religious chiefs have to convince potential followers that they each have a soul.

Where is it?

p.188

It resides [cannot say “lives”, in place of “resides” because this would imply that a soul can die] within each human, apparently, and continues when the physical body dies.

When does it come into existence?

Now this is a good one. The issue is when does the ephemeral soul associate with a human body. Is this at the point of birth or is it at conception? Maybe neither of these, but perhaps at some other stage during foetal, physical or mental development? There is potentially an issue with abortion, or miscarriage. Does the soul hook up at some stage after conception, but before the expected time of birth? This conjecture is only relevant if there is a soul.

Who has a soul?

Do animals other than humans have souls? If not, at what point in human evolution were souls attached to the body? Were souls only given to modern humans (MH)? MH have been around for 300,000 years. But their cousins, the Neanderthals have some similar characteristics to MH. They were bipedal. They had large brains, generally larger than MHs. And arguably Neanderthals had some form of spiritual belief. So, were they given souls in some mysterious way? What about all the intelligent, bipedal hominids who preceded both MH and Neanderthals: Homo Erectus; Homo Heidelbergensis; Homo Ergaster and Homo Hablis, to name but a few. Did these have souls too? If non-MH had souls, do they still exist somewhere in a heavenly, or hellish type location?

Perhaps though the provision of souls is dependent on brain size. The size choice imposed by the supreme deity could limit souls to MH and maybe Neanderthals. But if brains are vital to the decision awkward issues can arise. What effect, if any, does abortion, or any other cause of death of a foetus developing in a potential mother’s womb, have on the soul? What happens with MH who are born with much reduced mental capacity, because of some form of physical disability? If they do not have a soul that is doubly unfair situation. They would be denied not only a “normal” human life but also a hereafter one.

There is not just a sole question to be answered about souls, there’s lots of issues.

Heaven and Hell

Heaven

p.189

Heavens above! Heaven is a place of eternal peace - apparently. It’s where the souls reside of those rated good enough to get through its pearly gates. In heaven these souls are said to enjoy peace forever. Do we want everlasting peace, and not much else? Is “peace”, interspersed with regular God worship, all there is to be had from achieving heavenly status? Are humans to eternally forgo all the pleasures they enjoy once heavenly eminence is attained? - No eating, drinking, sex, TV, cinema, theatre, dinner parties, computer games, reading, cooking, rugby, football, badminton, fishing, cycling, walking, or bridge, and no texting or even gardening. Holidays at exotic locations or any location would be a no-no. Heaven has never even been reported as having a golf club, although God may have his own personal one. Do heavenly rules allow people, or their souls, to chat to family and friends? We don’t know, but this seems unlikely. Apparently, there’s no laughing either in Heaven. It’s all devoutly serious. Even smiles are frowned upon. Frowns are frowned upon too. Boring is too weak a word for heaven, a new word must be invented.

Artists, when picturing heaven, traditionally depict people in saintly mode encapsulated within glowing clouds. These righteous persons are usually shown with clasped hands pointing reverently upwards. Their fingers are sometimes aimed at a stern looking, white haired man glowering down from a cloud. The salvationista have a slightly vacant, puzzled look on their faces. Perhaps it’s just dawned on them what heaven is all about. How many weeks can the average person last in this traditional pious pose? Surely not for ever and ever.

Many religions, including pagan ones, have more inviting “heavens” than the Christian one. The Vikings version, Valhalla, with its promise of endless quaffing, singing and wenching would surely win any vote when the dull, solemn, reverential Christian Heaven was the alternative on the electoral card. Even women may prefer Valhalla. They could become beautiful Valkyries, riding white horses, singing Wagner, and choosing the fate of men in battle. Women with absolute power over men, maybe a feminist dream come true! Come back Odin, all is forgiven. We really need a religion that gives a wide choice of afterlives, just as proposed in Chapter 21, Design a God.

With a nod to dyslexia and pluralism, those Christian theologians might have almost got it right. Rather than removing to a place of eternal peace, i.e. Heaven, we in fact revert to the eternal pieces from which our body matter is composed. When we die we are usually cremated. Our bodies return to dust and eventually further decay into particles less than dust. These particles, in new molecular forms, find their way into the earth, air and cosmos itself. We are all composed of fundamental, basic atoms and sub-atomic particles, exactly as stars, planets and inter-stellar matter. As Carl Sagan once said: “We are star stuff.” As star stuff, all the pieces that make up us are in essence eternal. We may not be at peace but we are certainly in pieces.

Hell

Hell’s bells! We’ve had Heaven, but what about Hell? This is meant to be a place worse than Heaven, although there could be some contention about this. Traditionally, Hell is where bad souls go to because their bodily precursors had not towed a goodly line. It’s a hot location where the damned are eternally tortured, apparently. The King James Bible, Old Testament, mentions Sheol, on 31 occasions. Sheol is translated as “Hell”, mostly, but also refers to “the pit”. Modern versions of the Bible try to be slightly less frightening; for these Sheol is “the grave”. Tortured in the grave though is not any better than being tortured in Hell.

p.190

In that old favourite Bible version, the King James (KJV), Revelation 21:8 makes it terrifyingly clear what will happen to those who do not follow Biblical instruction to the letter: “But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and whoremongers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death.” If strictly applied this will really reduce the numbers going to heaven in a very big way. At the same time Hell is in danger of being clogged up. We may not have too many “sorcerers”, unless this is a miss-spelling and in fact refers to people who place their tea cup on a saucer, but in human history there’s been lots and lots of unbelievers and idolaters, then the ‘abominable’ can be a broad church, personal viewpoint dependent. We also have ‘liars’, which person can claim they have never, ever, told a fib? Hell must be a really big place and the heating bill enormous to cope with, what is probably the vast majority of humanity. Islam refers to Jahannam, a sort of Hell. Here, in the afterlife, evildoers are punished on a scale commensurate with how bad they’ve been in life, that seems a bit fairer than the Christian Hell where you get the full works regardless, although Dante thinks there are 9 levels of Hell. In Islam at the end of creation the dead will be raised. The soul is then weighed in terms of good or bad. Then souls have to pass over a test bridge to paradise. This widens for the good and narrows to a knife edge for the bad. Falling off this bridge is not a sensible thing to do, it’s straight to Jahannam.

There is no mention of hand-rails, so it obviously gets extremely difficult if the bridge is very narrow and if the soul does not have a good sense of balance. Infidels even suffer in the grave, before going on to Jahannam after judgement day.

Apparently, the soul of a dead infidel is wrenched from its body then interrogated by the angels Munkar and Nakir. Being infidels, they are certain to fail the interrogation and then suffer torment in the grave. Eventually they are eternally physically and spiritually tortured in Jahannam.

p.191

In the Jewish tradition there is place called Gehinnom (see also Chapter 16). This is akin to a very superior washing-machine for the soul. Baddish souls are thoroughly cleansed in Gehinnom. The sparkling clean soul is then ready for entry to heaven. Gehinnom may not be very pleasant for the soul. It has to endure a full wash cycle - on high heat and fast spin for the really bad soul. But this is only to be endured for quite a limited time. It is as nothing when compared to the Christian Hell or Islamic Jahannam. Dante’s vision of Hell deserves explaining. His 14th century epic poem sets out nine circles of Hell (inferno in Italian, Dante’s native tongue). Each succeeding circle represents an increase in wickedness. Which circle souls end up in depends on what bads people did in life. In the first circle souls tick over in Limbo - not too bad at all. This one is for the unbaptised and those pagans who have been quite good. Treachery is reckoned the most heinous sin and for that people land up in circle 9. You just don’t want to be end up there.

Here are the 9 in order of degree of badness from not so bad to appalling: First Circle - Limbo; Second Circle - Lust; Third Circle - Gluttony; Fourth Circle - Greed; Fifth Circle - Wrath; Sixth Circle - Heresy; Seventh Circle - Violence; Eighth Circle - Fraud; Ninth Circle - Treachery.

Heaven and Hell, a key problem

Imagine someone getting into heaven and looking down on a friend, or maybe family member, who had fallen foul of Revelation 21:8. Says the heavenista: “Look, there’s George in absolute agony again.” How can that person be serenely content in heaven, enjoying eternal peace, knowing that others are suffering eternal agony, sometimes for very minor misdemeanours? Is this evidence of a kind and loving God?

The Bible - again

p.192

The Bible, in spite of all the problems identified in Chapter 18, is at the heart of Christian faith. Much of it is also central to Judaism and Islam. All three great Abrahamic religions believe the Bible stems from God and is a divine guide to how people should live their lives, although the three religions do dispute the meaning of some parts, for example, Christians think Jesus is the son of God while both Jews and Islamists believe he was a prophet. Apart from this issue, there are quite large chunks of the Bible that are tricky to decode by present day readers. For example, when it comes to the ten commandments, very few of the texting generation have any difficulty in avoiding the temptation of coveting their: “…neighbour’s… manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass” (Exodus 20:17). Not many present-day neighbour’s have man and maid servants. Oxen are a bit thin on the ground too nowadays. All neighbours have asses though, some are asses too, but possibly the biblical writer did not intend the alternative, modern day meaning of “ass”. So, because most of the Bible was written several thousand years ago, interpretation of its meaning in a current context has been an ongoing issue. Some Christians, particularly Creationists, take a radical view. They maintain that all the words of the Bible are from God and therefore should be taken literally. This, they think, applies to the entirety of both New and Old Testaments, regardless of what Darwin, evolution, cosmology, science generally, simple human compassion or common sense says. Are the Creationists correct? Is the entire Bible the word of God? If it is, how far should it be literally interpreted and applied now, in modern times?

There is no uniform agreement as to the answer to this conundrum in the broad Christian community. Disputation on matters of theology; particularly Biblical interpretation; have often been violent in nature. Violent religious conflict is almost a defining feature of Christianity, up to the middle ages at least. These vicious disputes were not only with those of other beliefs but also with Christians who may have held slightly different views of Christianity.

1,700 years ago, the prolific Roman historian Ammianus Marcellinus in his “The Later Roman Empire” said: “No wild beasts are so deadly to humans as most Christians are to each other.” Marcellinus was even handed though, alongside his critique of Christians, he also had a go at Roman Emperor Julian’s paganism. Julian was particularly keen to placate Roman Gods through large scale animal sacrifice. Marcellinus said that Julian “drenched the altars with blood of an excessive number of victims, sometimes offering up a hundred oxen at once…”

Ancient language translation, is it right?

Dispute between Christians can also arise from mid-translation. There can be argument about the correctness of the translation of Biblical text from ancient Hebrew. Splitting hairs and holding strongly opposing views is a favourite human pastime. That is why we have a multitudinous line up of Christian religions (see Chapter 17). Although Christianity probably takes first prize, factionisation is not confined to Christians. It’s common currency in other major Abrahamic religions. While each religious faction may dissent from each other, for one reason or another, they all have in common their certainty. They each know, without doubt, that their particular religion or religious sect is the correct one. Theirs’s is the only one that really knows what God means to convey through his Biblical or other sacred text.

How do we decide for ourselves which of the wide variety of biblical based “truths” we should accept, if any at all? We need to go straight to source for clarity on some of the central issues here. It’s time to listen in again on another conversation between God and his chief hench-angel, Gabriel. On this occasion, we hear their discourse on the key topics of creation and the role of Jesus. These two biblical issues, one from the Old and the other from the New Testament, are closely linked.

Of all the archangels, Gabriel is renowned as the guardian of truth. It has always puzzled Gabriel that God made him his chief confidant despite knowing this.

The exchange here takes place quite some time after God created his cosmic golf course.

God: “Gabriel, I’ve quite enjoyed playing golf with you these last three million years, though I must say you don’t seem to make any improvement. You still drive off with a bent arm and your putting is appalling. However, that aside, I find I need something more than golf to occupy me.”

Gabriel, in a humble, contrite tone: “Sorry about my golf Lord, what are you considering now?”

God: “Well, I’m adding something to my original grand plan, which was to start up a new universe. Now, it will be one with the right conditions to allow humans to evolve.”

Gabriel: “Yes, Lord, I recall reading your great plan. Wasn’t your universe going to kick-off with an almighty, no pun intended Lord, explosion? Then 13.8 billion years later humans were to be on the scene? Incidentally, I wondered why you didn’t make that evolutionary period a nice round number, like 10 billion years. That would be much easier to remember.”

God: “Get with it Gabriel. If I made it “a nice round number” as you put it, humans might smell a rat. For the same reason you may have noticed that I’m going to make the circumference of a circle equal to 3.14159 and a bit of its diameter, instead of simply “3”. I know this may seem a little irrational, but you can’t make a Pi without having something left over. Also, humans will eventually evolve a very bright fellow with a name like a beer mug. If I didn’t have some odd numbers in the maths he certainly would catch on that someone had been fiddling the laws of physics and cosmology. One more thing. The kick-off for my new universe is not, “an almighty explosion.” That sounds like I might be blowing myself up.” “I’ve penned a clearer and snappier name than that - the Big Bang. Do try and get it right.”

Gabriel, somewhat bemused by this, hesitantly said: “I think I understand Lord.”

God, forcefully: “No you don’t. I can tell. Look, there’s going to be billions and billions of humans eventually. If they all got into heaven we wouldn’t have room to swing a cat up here, so, I can’t make it too easy for them to believe in me. Simple relational numbers will give the game away immediately. They’ll know then, without having to make any effort of faith, or even go to Sunday school, that I must exist. I want them to work hard at getting to know of me. I don’t want to set up the universe in a way that makes it too obvious that I exist. So, the numbers have got to be both difficult and awkward. There will even be something called quantum, which is so tricky it’s sort of magical. Even the beer mug guy will have trouble sorting that one out.”

Gabriel: “Ah, what brilliant logic, as always, Lord.”

God, unsuccessfully trying not to appear pleased at this praise, briskly carried on: “Now, to get back to my main point. I’ve decided I don’t want to wait 13.8 billion years to see what these interesting humans get up to, so I’m going to speed things up a bit.”

Gabriel injected: “So will you put everything together in, let’s say, 5 billion years - sorry (remembering God’s desire for odd numbers), I meant, say 5.76 billion years?”

God, slightly irritated at being interrupted: “No, I’ll do it even sooner than that. I’ll make the planet Earth and the first humans, Adam and Eve, within a week.” Gabriel responded in some awe: “Good God, even for you, isn’t that pushing it Lord. Besides, what about all the stars, planets, moons, comets etc in the universe. If it takes you a week to make just the Earth then the rest of the cosmos is going to take a lot longer. There will need to be lots of stars, not just those two lights in the firmament that you plan to make on the 4th day of creation (Gabriel had just read God’s plan at warp speed).”

God smirked knowingly at this and said: “Ah-ah, that’s where I have a cunning ruse! Of course, even the early, rather primitive humans, must be able look up into the sky and see twinkly lights that many generations later they will call stars. So, at first, I will do something simple and temporary to fool them. What I plan to do initially won’t take very long. I will put into geo-stationary orbit the right number of very large candles. These will be placed about 40 miles above the Earth surface. There will still be a slight atmosphere even at this height. But the wind will only be enough to make the candles flicker, not blow them out. The flickering will look like the twinkling that will later occur with stars.”

Gabriel: “Yes, pre-historic humans won’t be sophisticated enough to be able tell they are candles rather than stars, but later ones will with their telescopes and other instruments. What then Lord?”

God: “Well, we have many thousands of years before humans will be able to detect the candles. During that time, I’m going to have the entire host of angels gradually replacing the candles with proper stars. They’ll be set at the right distances and correct expansion speeds. When they’ve finished that job, I’ll get the angels to go to work on Earth. They’ll stuff dinosaur bones, the skeletons of ape-like humans, and other apparently ancient plants and animals into the Earth’s rock strata. With all this, by 2011, of Gregory’s calendar, human cosmologists will believe the universe is nearly 14 billion years old. Geologists, thinking they are so smart, with their isotropic and carbon dating techniques, will believe that the Earth is billions of years old too - 4.558 billion to be exact. It will be just those few astute Creationists who, despite all the apparent evidence to the contrary, will have sufficient faith to believe in the true cosmic timescale, i.e. 5,600 years. Gabriel: “If it’s only Creationists and their like who will get to come up to us, won’t that mean there will be a disproportionate number of Americans in heaven from the USA’s deep south?”

God stopped for a moment to gather his thoughts and then continued: “Coming back to the angels, they need something constructive to do.” “You can’t get much more constructive than building a star.” - Gabriel interjected knowingly. “Yess-s” said God, slightly distractedly. After pausing he continued: “At the moment those angels just lounge about putting on gold make-up, preening their feathers and strumming harps.” God frowned as he recalled the discordant harp playing that permeated every nook and cranny of heaven. Gabriel: “But you don’t have any…”

God, impatiently: “Yes, yes, I know I don’t have a nervous system. I was merely speaking meta… meta…”

“Metaphorically I think you mean Lord.” interposed Gabriel in a carefully polite tone.

“That’s the one.” replied God briskly. “Anyway, the angels can get on making the basic stars; planets; comets and asteroids. You archies (God’s collective term for his archangels) can have fun too. I’ll let you do the interesting stars: white dwarfs; black holes; red giants; pulsars; that sort of thing. You can even set off the odd supernova too.” - He added generously. “I will concentrate on galaxies. I’ve got some interesting shapes in mind, although for speed most will be simple spiral galaxies.”

Rubbing his hands together, self-satisfied, God added: “That’s this universe sorted. I’ve already written a detailed plan covering not just the first week but the first 5,600 years of Earth’s history. I want you to read it again now Gabriel, this time with more care, then give me your views.”

Gabriel, a few nanoseconds later (he was quite good at bending time): “I’ve read it again Lord and am a little concerned about some aspects.”

“Oh, what are they?” said God with some surprise.

Gabriel: “Well, for starters, right at the beginning I see Adam and Eve will be evicted from their home just because Adam eats an apple that you don’t want him to eat. Wouldn’t a simple ticking off be more appropriate than eviction?”

God: “The point is, it was Me who asked them not to eat that apple. They knew that but wanted to know more.”

Gabriel: “And so you think a much harsher punishment is warranted than this very minor misdemeanour would ordinarily attract?” God: “You’ve got it in one Gabriel. But of course, eviction is not sufficient punishment in this case. I give these two people everything. They defy me and are the first humans to do so. They are representing their entire race and should be more respectful.”

Gabriel: “Yes, I’ve noted that the whole of humanity is to be branded with “original sin” as a result of the A & E department operation. Surely Lord this is excessive in the extreme. Many billions of humans will be punished for something they didn’t do and had absolutely no control over. Even if Eve had picked every apple from your tree and baked apple pies with them all without offering you a single slice, that would not warrant what you are imposing.”

God: “Gabriel, I think you are forgetting just who is the Supreme Being here.”

Gabriel: “Deepest apologies Lord. Naturally I would never intend any offence. I would though like to make a further point.”

God, his fuse getting shorter, snapped: “And what is that?”

Gabriel hesitated, then replied in his most reasonable voice: “Adam and Eve and every human are to be punished for this “crime” but the perpetrator, Satan, thinly disguised as a snake, gets away scot free?”

God: “You know I have to be very careful when dealing with Fred (God always uses Satan’s first name). He’s a mischievous devil but very powerful. Were he and I to have a serious set-to the whole of reality would become twisted.”

Gabriel quickly picked up on this: “And your plan isn’t really twisted?”

Fortunately for Gabriel, God took his reply as a statement rather than a question: “Exactly, it’s actually quite straightforward. And on the original sin issue, if you’ve read the fine detail of my plan you would have noticed that I’ve allowed humans a way out.”

Gabriel: “Yes, I was coming to that. You intend that your only son, Jesus, should become a temporary human. He is then to suffer horrible torture and a slow and agonising death. What has any of this to do with Jesus? Someone steals one apple and as a result your son has to be killed as a form of expiation for all people, who also had nothing to do with it. I find this hard to understand Lord.”

God: “It makes for a great story and really impresses the humans. They talk about it for thousands of years afterwards. Humans even celebrate Jesus’ immaculate birthday every year. Admittedly, in time the birthday celebration mostly involves heavy duty shopping and debauchery, but I can live with that. And then there’s Easter with all those chocolate eggs and the consumption of billions of hot-cross buns. So this story has quite a following.”

Gabriel: “But you only have one son Jesus. He, apparently, adores you. Why hurt him?”

God: “Stop making such a fuss. Jesus is not only going to absolve mankind from the apple sin he’s got to go down to Earth and pass on righteous messages from me to these humans. In any case, he needs to get out and about more. The experience will toughen him up, but He’ll soon be over it. Why, I’m going to beam him back up here at the end of his Earthly visit, so he won’t have to worry about return travel arrangements.”

Gabriel felt compelled to continue despite being aware he was on dodgy ground: “Lord, yes I know you will raise Jesus up, but only after he’s been dead for three days! Why not do it three days before his intended death?”

God, impatiently and forcefully: “Gabriel, you really must try and see the bigger picture here. If I raised him before he died that would completely destroy the dramatic impact I’m trying to achieve. And then there would be no more Easters either. Think of all those people who will eventually work in the chocolate Easter egg and hot-cross bun industries. They would be without a job.”

Gabriel made a last desperate attempt to delay God’s plan and get Him to re-consider: “Lord, I know you’ve been working really hard recently. This plan of yours is…is…amazing and clearly the result of a lot of effort. I think you deserve a holiday. Let me knock up a planet with sandy beaches, warm blue seas, waving palm trees and mountain views in the background. You could chill out on the beach for a week. In the second week we could decamp to your galactic golf course and hold a tournament with all the archies.”

Gabriel decided that the point had come where he had to play his last high card: “You are very good at golf remember.” - he said with heavy emphasis.

God hesitated for a moment, then with a note of finality in his voice: “I’m much too busy to go on holiday, despite the temptation of winning the golf tournament. Gabriel, I know that you think my plan is nonsensical but you’ll see, it will all work out in the end. I’m planning some really big fireworks when the curtain eventually comes down on humankind. I think you will enjoy the spectacle.”

Gabriel, dispiritedly giving up said: “Thy will be done Lord” and very quietly, under his breath, added: “not only Will will be done; Jesus and other humans too.”

Islam refers to the religion of Muslims, who are the followers of Allah (God). Christians believe in Jesus Christ, whereas Muslims also see Jesus as an important prophet but not as the son of God. Rather, Muslims believe Muhammad to be God’s final prophet. The Qur’an is central to Islamic faith, as revealed to Muhammad over 23 years through the archangel Gabriel. Muslims believe Allah (God) is one and the same as the Abrahamic God worshipped by Jews and Christians, though the three religions interpret God’s message and requirements differently.

p.198

The resurrection of Jesus Christ is central to Christian faith. Christians believe that Jesus was crucified, died, and was buried, but rose from the dead three days later. This event, commemorated at Easter, represents victory over death and sin, and provides hope of eternal life for believers. The resurrection is seen as proof of Jesus’ divine nature and God’s power over death itself.

However, critics question various aspects of this narrative. Why wait three days? Why allow the suffering in the first place if resurrection was planned? And what about those who lived before Jesus or never heard of him - are they condemned through no fault of their own?

The story has persisted for over 2,000 years, inspiring billions while raising fundamental questions about justice, mercy, and the nature of salvation. Easter celebrations in 2018 and beyond continue this tradition, though for many the religious significance has been overshadowed by chocolate eggs and commercial interests.

Christians see the Bible as prophetic guidance, while Muslims view Muhammad as the final prophet, and Jews await the Messiah. Jesus is variously seen as: son of God by Christians; a prophet (but not divine) by Muslims; and not recognized as Messiah by Jews. Islam does not view Jesus as the son of God but as a significant prophet and servant of Allah (God). All three Abrahamic faiths - Judaism, Christianity and Islam - trace back to Abraham and worship the same God, though they differ fundamentally on interpretation, practice, and the role of various prophets and holy figures.
